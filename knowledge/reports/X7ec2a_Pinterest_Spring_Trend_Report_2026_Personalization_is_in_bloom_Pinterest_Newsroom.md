In: changing moods. Out: changing self. Pinterest’s Spring Trend Report reveals
a cultural shift away from perfectionism and reinvention toward brands,
foods and experiences that enhance self-expression, comfort, and positive
vibes. People want to feel good about their lives in 2026, not upend them.
The resetting of expectations is born out in Pinterest’s annual analysis of
what’s hot and what’s not, based not on expert guesses or fleeting internet
chatter, but on the future plans of over 600 million monthly active users.1
Unlike other lists, the Spring Trend Report taps into the collective mood
board of people actively searching, saving, and shaping what’s next—
offering a clear preview of where culture is headed, often before anyone
else sees it. On Pinterest, planning the future is personal. And even the
smallest choices can spark the biggest shifts.

Home & renovations: Curated comfort
and micro-makeovers
This year’s Spring Trend Report is proof that home isn’t about perfection.
It’s about showing off who you really are, right now. Gen Z and younger
Millennials aren’t waiting for the “dream house” or a big-budget reno; they’re
making rooms their own through bold color, vintage finds, and creative,
low-lift hacks. These trends reveal a generational shift: comfort over status,
playfulness over approval, and personal vibes over passing fads. This is the
season of micro-makeovers, where even a splash of paint or a reading nook
becomes a statement. If you’re ready to make your space feel unapologetically
you, this is your spring playbook.
The “all-white everything” era is giving way to a “my room, my rules” (+415%)2
mentality, proving that Pinners are rewriting the rules and transforming their
space into unapologetic showcases of self. People want bold, expressive
colors like “moody blue” and “vintage pink” to personalize their spaces.
Even kitchen cabinets are getting a splash of paint, with searches surging
for aesthetics like "Aubergine kitchen" (+495%). Vintage-meets-modern
kitchens are also on the rise, with “grandma core kitchen” up +545% and
“dark cottagecore kitchen” up +915%, as people lean into comfort, character
and a little nostalgia at the center of the home.
The smallest spaces are getting their moment this spring too. Searches for
“comfy reading chair small spaces” (+455%) and “closet reading nook for
adults” (+55%) are on the rise as people crave a cozy escape. And whether
it’s a sunny window seat or a secret corner, these snug retreats prove that
comfort can fit anywhere this season.

Food: Spring soups, not spring diets
This season, the kitchen is the new comfort zone. Pinterest users are ditching
spring detoxes and filling their dining rooms with creativity, friends and
cozy food with a bright, modern twist. Comfort-first cooking is on the rise,
with savory florals and veggie-based mains like “eggplant parmesan” on
the table. Bowls of clam chowder are up 300%, while punchy carbonara
buldak ramen is spicing up mealtimes. Sustainability is also on the menu,
as low-waste recipes for leftover spaghetti (+570%) and creative pantry
staples simplify home cooking.
The comfort trend is also seeping into the social scene: playful breakfast
grazing boards, whimsical balcony brunches, and cake picnics (+275%)
are turning everyday gatherings into colorful celebrations, without the
usual hosting stress.

Activities: Tiny sanctuaries and
intentional connection
Spring plans are getting more local, more doable, and more centered on
home. Pinners are looking for “micro escapes” that fit into real life: 10-
minute garden breaks, renter-friendly balcony makeovers, and tiny outdoor
sanctuaries. Searches for “garden inspiration ideas” are up 940%, and
“balcony makeover ideas” is up 165%, showing that even small outdoor
havens are becoming spring’s main stage.
When it comes to the social calendar, neighborhood potlucks, “bring-
a-bloom” flower parties, and easy hangouts like “backyard movie night
party” (+130%) are on the agenda. And for anyone craving a calmer pace,
mindful reset rituals are rising too, with searches like “sunday reset list”
(+65%) and “sunday reset aesthetic” (+55%) reflecting a spring that’s less
about doing the most and more about feeling your best.

Spring cleaning & organizing: Cleaning is the
new self-care
Spring cleaning is no longer a once-a-year marathon. It’s a series of small
resets that feel supportive, not punishing. People are searching for routines
that break the work into manageable moments, like “cleaning list by room step
by step” (+175%), plus satisfying “fresh start” organization inspiration such
as “fridge organization aesthetic” (+375%) and “laundry room organization
small space” (+390%). Sustainability is part of the story, too. Interest is
climbing in greener, refillable and DIY approaches like “natural cleaning”
(+545%) and “vinegar cleaning solution” (+165%). Even motivation is getting
a makeover, with “clean-tertainment” content like “before and after cleaning”
(+60%) and reset-themed inspiration (including “reset aesthetic,” +200%)
helping people turn maintenance into momentum.
